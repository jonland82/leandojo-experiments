\begin{abstract}
We place 18,852 declarations from nine major Lean formalization projects into
statement and proof embedding spaces anchored by a fixed sample of 10,000
LeanDojo theorem--proof pairs.  The frontier declarations are substantially
more isolated than the reference corpus: 62.7\% of their statement
neighborhood densities fall below the fifth percentile of LeanDojo's own
density distribution.  They are nevertheless not detached from the library.
LeanDojo statements selected as semantic neighbors have proofs that are, on
average, 0.0565 cosine points closer to the frontier proof than random
references.  The result supports a picture of frontier formalization as
peripheral but locally connected to established proof infrastructure.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Corpus.}
The reference set $R=\{1,\ldots,10{,}000\}$ is the seed-0 LeanDojo sample used
in the earlier semantic-geometry experiments.  The frontier set contains
declarations from OpenAI's Ten Proofs and Navier--Stokes/Euler repositories,
the complete FLT certificate, PFR, PNT+, Formalized Formal Logic,
Jordan--Sch\"onflies, Liquid Tensor, and Sphere Eversion.  Large projects are
represented by a seed-0 sample of approximately 2,500 declarations plus every
identified headline theorem; smaller projects are retained in full.  FLT is a
stratified 2,003-declaration sample of theorem cards, proof modules, and
definitions.  This balancing prevents the largest generated repositories from
determining the aggregate by sheer file count.

\paragraph{Frozen semantic map.}
For view $v\in\{s,p\}$, let $r_i^{(v)}\in\mathbb R^{1024}$ be the unit-normalized
embedding of LeanDojo reference $i$, where $s$ is theorem-statement text and
$p$ is proof text.  Let $x_j^{(v)}$ denote the corresponding embedding of
frontier declaration $j$.  All vectors use the same frozen Cohere Embed v4
model and prompts as the original experiment; neither the reference sample nor
its fitted clusters is changed.

For a frontier vector $x$ define its exact top-$k$ reference neighborhood and
local reference density by
\[
 N_k^{(v)}(x)=\operatorname*{arg\,topk}_{i\in R}
 \langle x^{(v)},r_i^{(v)}\rangle,
 \qquad
 d_k^{(v)}(x)=\frac1k\sum_{i\in N_k^{(v)}(x)}
 \langle x^{(v)},r_i^{(v)}\rangle .
\]
Cosine similarity is the inner product because the vectors have unit length.
For each reference vector, the same statistic is computed after excluding the
vector itself.  If $q_{.05}^{(v)}$ is the fifth percentile of those reference
densities, the indicator
\[
 I^{(v)}(x)=\mathbf 1\!\left[d_{10}^{(v)}(x)<q_{.05}^{(v)}\right]
\]
marks a declaration as locally sparse relative to LeanDojo.

To test whether statement proximity carries proof information, let
$S_j=N_{10}^{(s)}(x_j)$ and let $U_j$ be ten seed-0 random reference indices.
The transfer statistic is
\[
 \Delta_j=\frac1{10}\sum_{i\in S_j}
 \langle x_j^{(p)},r_i^{(p)}\rangle
 -\frac1{10}\sum_{i\in U_j}
 \langle x_j^{(p)},r_i^{(p)}\rangle .
\]
This is a simple global-random baseline, not the context-matched baseline used
in the earlier within-LeanDojo experiment.

\paragraph{Results.}
At $k=10$, the frontier statement-density mean is 0.6717, compared with 0.7662
for reference declarations.  The corresponding proof values are 0.7030 and
0.7971.  Consequently, 62.7\% of frontier statements and 60.7\% of frontier
proofs fall below their reference fifth-percentile thresholds.  Isolation is
therefore a corpus-level shift, not a property of only a few famous endpoints.

The project ordering is interpretable.  The diverse 2026 Ten Proofs bundle and
the new PDE corpus are the most peripheral, while logic and established
topological developments lie closer to material already represented in
Mathlib.  Headline results need not be the most isolated parts of their
projects: the FLT endpoint has nearest-statement cosine 0.7807, whereas a
Navier--Stokes breakdown endpoint is as low as 0.5611.  This distinction is why
the project cloud is more informative than one dot per famous theorem.

Across all declarations, $\mathbb E[\Delta_j]=0.0565$ (median 0.0524).  Thus the
frontier is sparse relative to LeanDojo but remains locally tethered to it:
nearby established statements tend to have more relevant proof representations
than arbitrary library statements.  Direct overlap between the ten nearest
statement and proof neighbors is low (mean Jaccard index 0.028), consistent
with the earlier finding that statements and proofs share useful local
structure without inducing the same global geometry.  Figure~\ref{fig:spaces}
shows these two views together, allowing direct visual comparison of how the
same projects occupy statement and proof space without changing the
quantitative analysis.

\paragraph{Interpretation and limits.}
Major formalizations generally lie near the edge of the older LeanDojo corpus,
yet remain built from locally recognizable semantic and procedural material.
They are therefore useful out-of-distribution retrieval tests rather than
wholly unrelated corpora.  The statement result is primary: frontier proofs
were extracted statically, while LeanDojo records tactic traces.  Project
comparisons also confound toolchain, domain, authorship, and AI use; embeddings
measure textual semantics, the reference samples only 10,000 declarations, and
the random baseline ignores source context.  Confirmation requires native
elaboration, dependency cones, and matched controls.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The experiment freezes repository commits, sampling seed, preprocessing,
embedding configuration, and a hard AWS spending cap.  Inputs, embeddings,
nearest-neighbor arrays, code, and machine-readable results are retained under
\texttt{experiments/frontier-formalizations/}.  Successful inference calls,
including an abandoned larger pilot, cost an estimated \$0.60--\$0.96.
