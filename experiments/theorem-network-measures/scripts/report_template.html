Three questions, three measures

Throughout, $i$ denotes one target theorem–proof record and $\log$ is the natural logarithm.

Dependency mass

How reliant is it on prior mathematics?

$$D_\alpha(i)=\sum_{u\in A(i)}\alpha^{d(u,i)},\quad \alpha=0.5$$

An edge $u\to v$ means declaration $v$ uses $u$ in its kernel type or proof term. $A(i)$ is the set of upstream declarations that can reach $i$; $d(u,i)$ is the shortest path length. The discount $\alpha=0.5$ downweights remote ancestors. The displayed score is $\log(1+D_{0.5}(i))$.

Effective neighborhood

How connected is it semantically?

$$x_i=\frac{e_i}{\lVert e_i\rVert_2},\qquad w_{ij}=\max(0,x_i^\top x_j-\tau)$$ $$p_{ij}=\frac{w_{ij}}{\sum_\ell w_{i\ell}}$$ $$N_{\rm eff}(i)=\exp\!\left(-\sum_jp_{ij}\log p_{ij}\right)$$

$e_i$ is the Cohere Embed v4 vector and $x_i$ its unit normalization. Indices $j$ and $\ell$ range over the exact top-100 neighbors; $\tau$ is the view-specific random-pair cosine threshold. Thus $w_{ij}$ is excess cosine similarity, $p_{ij}$ its normalized share, and $N_{\rm eff}$ the entropy-equivalent neighbor count; it is set to zero if all weights vanish.

Structural complexity

How involved is its recorded proof?

$$H_i=-\sum_tq_{it}\log q_{it},\qquad z(y)=\frac{y-\bar y}{\operatorname{sd}(y)}$$ $$\begin{aligned}C(i)=\tfrac14\big[&z(\log(1+s_i))+z(\log(1+r_i))\\&+z(h_i)+z(H_i)\big]\end{aligned}$$

$s_i$ is tactic-step count, $r_i$ direct kernel-reference count, and $h_i$ maximum dependency depth. $q_{it}$ is the fraction of steps using tactic head $t$, making $H_i$ tactic-head entropy. In $z$, $\bar y$ and $\operatorname{sd}(y)$ are the population mean and standard deviation over all 10,000 targets.

Conceptual takeaways

Position has multiple axes

Using a lot of mathematics, resembling many other theorems, and having an involved recorded proof are different properties. No single notion of “importance” subsumes all three.

Reliance is not connectedness

Dependency mass correlates negatively with statement and proof connectedness. Deeply supported theorems are often specialized endpoints rather than semantic hubs.

Claims cluster more than procedures

Statements have {{STATEMENT_NEFF}} median effective neighbors versus {{PROOF_NEFF}} for proofs. Mathematical claims reuse common forms more readily than tactic implementations do.

Proof strategy retains freedom

Statement and proof connectedness agree only moderately, $\rho={{CROSS_VIEW_RHO}}$. Similar-looking statements constrain proof style without determining it.

Involved proofs are often bespoke

Complexity and proof connectedness have $\rho={{PROOF_COMPLEXITY_RHO}}$. Structurally involved proofs tend to have fewer close procedural analogues.

The retrieval frontier is specialized

High-reliance, low-connectedness theorems may be hardest for retrieval: they need broad infrastructure while offering few genuinely close examples.

A two-axis map

Low reliance · connectedReusable or basic theorem families
Low reliance · isolatedUnusual but elementary results
High reliance · connectedMature specialized families
High reliance · isolatedBespoke endpoints atop deep infrastructure

Scope: these scores describe position in this Mathlib snapshot and this embedding geometry—not intrinsic mathematical depth. Dependency mass includes formalization machinery, while semantic isolation can reflect notation and proof-writing style.
